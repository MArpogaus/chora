## 1. Mechanisms that let visible flaws through

1. **Conformance to a wrong spec.** ux-hud-sprint.md specified one host with a sentence balloon and a separate chat
   panel, and the build matched it. A spec review cannot find a spec error (friction 29 is the root, 28 its symptom).
2. **No target experience.** No brief said "this must read as the Office Assistant on XP", and nobody had a reference
   of the real balloon (#FFFFE1, tail to the head, input and buttons inside the one balloon, the figure never
   rescaled). "Findings" with no target defaults to "does it work".
3. **Severity defined by tasks.** Block/major/minor asks "can a person finish the task". A stretched figure blocks no
   task, so it ranks minor, and rule 5 (MEDIUM/LOW to the backlog) plus the first judge's "at most 10 findings" cap
   push it off the list.
4. **Whole screens, not crops.** A 1280x720 frame shown whole to a model is downsampled; a 3 px clip on a 70 px sprite
   vanishes. Nobody cropped a surface or zoomed in.
5. **Words before pixels.** Reviewers read the diff, the worker's report and the note first. A note that says "the
   balloon shows the sentence" primes the eye to see exactly that.
6. **Builder captures, builder narrates, PM publishes.** The ux-chat worker made and named the captures; the PM posted
   them as "HUD looks" with no checklist. Nobody independent stood between builder and user.
7. **No capture matrix.** xp at 1280x720 only, nothing at scale 2, no state strip (idle → speaking → chat open →
   question) side by side, which is where "two surfaces" jumps out.
8. **No mechanical layout invariants.** Overlapping surfaces, a surface outside the work area, a sprite scaled
   non-uniformly, text past its box: all measurable, none a gate cell.
9. **Throughput mood.** 16 merges in 5 h, one review round, progress counted in merges. Under that pressure a pixel
   flaw ranks below a working feature.

The construct lane that failed only in the full gate (F8) and the T3 cell are the same failure in tests: passing alone
counts as passing. `--no-verify` (friction 27) shows that a rule kept only in prose is skipped under pressure. Both
need a mechanical stop, not more prose.

## 2. What must change so UX pass 2 and the showcase catch everything visible

- **Reference sheet per look** (design/looks/ref/, review only, never shipped), 3-6 captures or exact descriptions.
  xp: the XP balloon and the Office Assistant (one balloon, tail to the head, input and buttons inside, the figure at
  native size, idle animation in place). mc: a TV launcher at 3 m. construct: its own glass, approved by the user.
- **Capture matrix from one script** (`tests/showcase.sh`, scene clock): 4 looks × 2 themes × {1280x720 @1, 2560x1440
  @2} × 8 states (idle, speaking, working, question, chat open, muted, notice, refusal), a 2x crop of every surface,
  3 short clips (open, expand, question). This is also the showcase the user asked for: one set of files for all.
- **Layout audit gate cell.** A test-only `hud.layout` method dumps surface rects and image draws (source and drawn
  size). It fails on overlapping helper surfaces, a surface outside the work area, a non-uniform or fractional image
  scale, and text overflowing its box. Write it red first: it must fail on today's paperclip.
- **Pixels first, words last.** The reviewer describes each crop before reading any note, then lists every
  difference from the reference, then marks the look's checklist pass/fail per item with a capture name, then reads
  the diff.
- **Separation.** The builder never reviews its own pixels; its self-check is required but does not count. Main paths
  get two reviewers: a checklist reviewer (look doc plus reference) and a "first look" reviewer who gets only the
  captures and the look's people paragraph, no spec, and answers in 10 lines: "what looks wrong, foreign or cheap?".
- **Visual severity.** A visible flaw on a main-path state is MAJOR and blocks the merge of a UI lane. No cap on
  visual findings. Off main paths: the known-issues list, with the capture.
- **The user's taste is the arbiter.** A taste question (Tahoma 14 vs XP's 11 px; paperclip click opens or mutes) goes
  as two captures side by side and one line. The showcase gets a per-look "approved by the user" mark, an rc criterion.
- **The PM looks before posting.** No media reaches the status page without the checklist pass, or it says "unreviewed".

## 3. Did the first reflection's rules hit the right causes?

Right, and adopted: merge train, lanes by file, red first at merge, window audit, the honest-state item. They target
throughput and functional regressions. Missing: anything about how things look. Its table of recurring findings has
no visual row, and it praised the UX loop by the blocks it found without asking what it missed. Wrong for UI work:
- "At most 10 findings, at most 40 lines" suppresses exactly the small visual findings the user wants. Keep it for
  code reviews only.
- Rule 5 (one fix round; MEDIUM/LOW to the backlog) parks pixel flaws. UI lanes need polish rounds until the
  checklist passes, at most 3, then the user decides.
- `--fast` worker gates move full-run-only failures (F8, T3) into the PM's train, where a red costs a bisect. A lane
  touching tests/, a shared crate or core input/state/ipc runs the full gate itself.
- Rule 8 "dropped (kept, not followed)" deleted nothing: the charter grew from 1,091 to 1,139 lines. Delete the text.
- The 11-18 rc date did not foresee a redesign of four helper surfaces (U3, friction 29). It holds only if the work is
  cut to "one surface per look, collapsed and expanded"; otherwise 11-20.

## 4. Design documents per look: content and the KISS risk

The xp.md draft has the right shape. Each look doc should have these seven parts, in this order, in 100 lines or fewer:
(1) people and usage, ≤8 lines; (2) links to its reference sheet; (3) the helper surface table, one row per state,
collapsed and expanded; (4) measurable rules (sizes, margins, colours, scale); (5) do and don't, ≤6 each; (6) the
review checklist, ≤10 items, each checkable on a capture; (7) open questions, each with an owner. Risks:
- Six drafts next to design-principles, four style guides and ux-hud-sprint.md are three sources of truth. Mark
  ux-hud-sprint.md §2-4 superseded today, or each reviewer picks the note that fits.
- Builders read tables and checklists, not prose. A line that cannot be checked on a capture does not belong.
- Open questions let builders guess, and a guess becomes the spec. xp.md has five; the user answers them before a
  builder starts.
- The helper surface crosses the HUD area and the look area. One owner (the HUD/chat worker), with the look doc as the
  acceptance test, or U3 gets fixed four different ways.

## 5. Process load on the PM

The PM merges, resolves conflicts, writes briefs, posts media, logs friction, keeps backlog, state and answers, and
drafts rules. Quality slipped at its own checkpoint (five posts of a flawed capture), and the same evening it opened
new scope: L1 item 4 (paste upload and issue text) and the status page rework. Both are features, against the freeze.
- Workers rebase and resolve their own conflicts; the PM runs the train and bisects. At most 3 trains a day.
- The freed time goes to looking: 10 minutes per train on the showcase crops.
- One in, one out: a new charter rule lands only with an equal deletion. Target ≤700 lines by 11-13.
- Briefs are filled-in templates. A brief over 40 lines means a design question is still open.
- L1 item 4 and the status page rework wait until after the rc. L1 logging (rc 9a) stays.
