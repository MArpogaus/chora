## 1. Mechanisms that let visible flaws through

1. **Reviewing for conformance to a wrong spec.** ux-hud-sprint.md specified one host with a sentence
   balloon and a separate chat panel. Reviewers checked the build against it, and the build matched. A
   spec review cannot find a spec error. Friction 29 is the root and friction 28 is its symptom.
2. **No target experience.** No brief said "this must read as the Office Assistant on XP". No reference
   image of the real XP balloon (#FFFFE1, tail to the character, input and buttons inside the one balloon,
   the figure never rescaled) was given to anyone. "Findings" with no target defaults to "does it work".
3. **Severity is defined by tasks.** Block/major/minor means "can a person finish the task". A stretched
   figure blocks no task, so it ranks minor. Then rule 5 (MEDIUM/LOW to the backlog) and the first
   judge's "at most 10 findings, ranked" cap push it off the list.
4. **Whole-screen images, not crops.** A 1280x720 frame shown whole to a model is downsampled. A 3 px
   clip at the edge of a 70 px sprite is invisible at that size. Nobody cropped a surface or zoomed in.
5. **Words before pixels.** Reviewers got the diff, the worker's report and the design note, and then the
   capture. A note that says "the balloon shows the sentence" primes the reviewer to see exactly that.
6. **Builder captures, builder narrates, PM publishes.** The ux-chat worker made the captures and named
   them. The PM posted them as "HUD looks" without a checklist. Nobody independent stood between builder
   and user.
7. **No capture matrix.** xp was captured at 1280x720 only; nothing at scale 2; no state strip (idle →
   speaking → chat open → question) side by side, which is where "two surfaces" jumps out.
8. **No mechanical layout invariants.** Overlap of surfaces, a surface outside the work area, a sprite
   scaled non-uniformly, or text past its box can all be measured. None of them is a gate cell, unlike
   the window audit that the first judge proposed for window loss.
9. **Throughput mood.** 16 merges in 5 h; reviews capped at one round; the PM measured progress in merges.
   A reviewer under that pressure ranks a pixel flaw below a working feature.

The construct branch that failed only in the full gate (F8, ux-construct) and the T3 cell are the same
failure in tests: a check that passes alone is treated as passing. And `--no-verify` (friction 27) shows
that a rule only in prose is skipped under pressure. Both need a mechanical stop, not more prose.

## 2. What must change so the next UX pass and the showcase catch everything visible

- **Reference sheet per look** (design/looks/ref/): 3-6 reference captures or exact descriptions of the
  target, for review only, never shipped. xp: the XP balloon and the Office Assistant (one balloon,
  tail to the head, input and buttons inside it, the figure at native size, idle animations in place).
  mc: a TV launcher at 3 m. construct: its own glass captures, approved by the user. tiling: waybar/rofi.
- **Capture matrix from one script** (`tests/showcase.sh`, deterministic scene clock): 4 looks × 2 themes
  × {1280x720 @1, 2560x1440 @2} × 8 states (idle, speaking, working, question, chat open, muted,
  notice, refusal), plus a crop of every surface at 2x, plus 3 short clips (open, expand, question).
  The same matrix is the showcase the user asked for. Builders, reviewers and the user read the same files.
- **Layout audit gate cell.** A test-only `hud.layout` method dumps every surface rect and every image
  draw (source size, drawn size). It fails on overlap of two helper surfaces, a surface outside the
  output's work area, a non-uniform or fractional image scale, and text overflowing its box. Write it red
  first against today's xp (it must fail on the paperclip).
- **Reviewer brief: pixels first, words last.** Step 1: for each crop, describe what you see, before
  reading any note. Step 2: compare with the reference and list every difference. Step 3: walk the look's
  checklist and mark each item pass/fail with a capture name. Only then read the diff.
- **Separation.** The builder never reviews its own pixels, and its self-check does not count. Main paths
  get two reviewers: (a) the checklist reviewer with the look doc and the reference; (b) a "first look"
  reviewer who gets only the captures and the look's people paragraph, no spec, and answers in 10 lines:
  "what looks wrong, foreign or cheap?". (b) is what caught nothing today because it did not exist.
- **Severity for visuals.** A visible flaw on a main-path state is MAJOR and blocks the merge of a UI lane.
  No cap on visual findings. Off main paths: the known-issues list with the capture.
- **The user's taste is the arbiter.** A taste question (Tahoma 14 vs XP's 11 px, paperclip click opens or
  mutes) goes to the user as two captures side by side and one line. The showcase page gets a per-look
  "approved by the user" mark. That mark is an rc criterion.
- **The PM looks before posting.** No media goes to the status page without the checklist pass, or it is
  labelled "unreviewed".

## 3. Did the first reflection's rules hit the right causes?

Right: merge train, lanes by file, red first as a merge check, window audit, honest-state item. They target
throughput and functional regressions, and they were adopted.

Missing: anything about how things look. Its table of recurring findings has no visual row. It praised
the UX loop by the count of blocks it found and never asked what it missed.

Wrong or harmful for UI work:
- "At most 10 findings, ranked; at most 40 lines" suppresses exactly the small visual findings the user now
  wants. Keep it for code reviews; drop it for UI reviews.
- Rule 5 (one fix round; MEDIUM/LOW to backlog) sends pixel flaws to the backlog. For UI lanes, polish
  rounds continue until the checklist passes, at most 3, then the user decides.
- `--fast` worker gates move full-run-only failures (F8, T3) to the PM's train, where a red costs a bisect.
  A lane that touches tests/, a shared crate or core input/state/ipc runs the full gate itself.
- Rule 8 "dead weight dropped (kept, not followed)": the text was not deleted. The charter grew from 1,091
  to 1,139 lines. Every reader still loads it. Delete it.
- The rc date 11-18 did not foresee a redesign of four helper surfaces (U3, friction 29). It holds only if
  the per-look surface work is cut to "one surface, collapsed and expanded" and nothing more; otherwise 11-20.

## 4. Design documents per look: content and the KISS risk

The xp.md draft is the right shape: people, usage, one-surface state table, hard rules ("never stretched"),
do/don't, a measurable checklist, open questions. Each look doc must contain, in this order, ≤100 lines:
1. People and usage (≤8 lines); 2. the target reference (links to ref/); 3. the helper surface table: one row
per state, collapsed and expanded; 4. measurable rules (sizes, margins, colours, scale); 5. do/don't
(≤6 each); 6. the review checklist (≤10 items, each checkable on a capture); 7. open questions, each with an owner.
Risks:
- Six drafts (524 lines) next to design-principles, four style guides and ux-hud-sprint.md give three
  sources of truth. Mark ux-hud-sprint.md sections 2-4 superseded by looks/ today, or reviewers will pick
  whichever note fits.
- Builders read the table and the checklist, not prose. Keep any line that cannot be checked on a capture
  out of the doc.
- Open questions in a draft let builders guess, and a guess becomes the spec. xp.md has five open
  questions. The user answers them before a builder starts (one message, captures attached).
- Lane ownership: the helper surface crosses the HUD area and the look area. Name one owner (the HUD/chat
  worker) and make the look doc its acceptance test. Otherwise U3 is fixed four different ways.

## 5. Process load on the PM

The PM merges, resolves conflicts, writes briefs, posts media, logs friction, keeps backlog, state and
answers, and drafts rules. Quality slipped at the PM's own checkpoint (five posts of a flawed capture).
In the same evening it opened new scope: L1 item 4 (paste upload plus GitHub issue text) and the status
page rework. Both are features, against the rc freeze.
- Workers rebase and resolve their own conflicts. The PM only runs the train and bisects.
- At most 3 trains a day. The PM's freed time goes to looking: 10 minutes per train at the showcase crops.
- One in, one out: a new charter rule only lands with an equal deletion. Target ≤700 lines by 11-13.
- Briefs come from the templates and are filled in, not written fresh. A brief longer than 40 lines is a sign
  that a design question is still open.
- Park L1 item 4 and the status-page rework after the rc. L1 logging (9a) stays.
